## Who this is for

- **Professionals across any function** — marketing, ops, HR, finance, support, analysis, teaching — who feel a step behind and aren't sure where to start.
- **Capable people who've stalled** — you use AI fine, but "building something with it" feels like it belongs to someone else.
- **Career-changers and future-proofers** who want to be genuinely fluent, not just busy with tools.

## The gap that's probably holding you

The biggest barrier to going further isn't between beginner and expert. It's the jump from **using AI well for yourself** to **building something with it for others** — and it has almost nothing to do with skill. It's a self-permission gap: the distance between what you know and who you believe does this kind of thing.

That's good news, because it means the fix isn't another course. It's permission, proximity to a peer one stage ahead, and the right question at the right moment. The [framework](framework/six-stages-for-professionals.md) explains exactly what moves it.

## The six stages

Every playbook in this series shares one diagnosis of where you actually are — by how it feels, not by tools deployed:

> Nomad → Observer → Dabbler → Integrator → **Architect** → Innovator

The **Architect** stage — capable, able to see what you'd build, not yet committed to being the person who builds it — is where most professionals stall longest. Full cut, and what moves each stage, in **[framework/six-stages-for-professionals.md](framework/six-stages-for-professionals.md)**. Canonical model + stage locator: **[ai-adoption-model](https://github.com/VictorOsondu/ai-adoption-model)**.

## Start here

1. **Locate yourself** with the two-minute [stage locator](https://github.com/VictorOsondu/ai-adoption-model/blob/main/framework/stage-locator.md). It tells you what's actually holding you — usually not what you'd guess.
2. **Find your role** in the [role paths](templates/role-paths.md) and take the one safe *Improve* win listed for it.
3. **If you landed at Integrator or Architect,** the work isn't a course — it's a question: *whose problem do you keep noticing that nobody's solving?*

# Source-Verification Checklist

Last reviewed: 2026-07-24. See [sources and verification notes](../sources.md).

Use this whenever AI provides facts, statistics, citations, legal authorities, research references, quotes, code, calculations, or claims that matter.

## Verify the Thing, Not the Confidence

AI can sound certain while being wrong. Asking the same AI to confirm its own answer is not verification.

## Checklist

- [ ] I found the source outside the AI tool.
- [ ] The source exists and is accessible.
- [ ] The source says what the AI claims it says.
- [ ] The date, jurisdiction, version, or edition is current for my use.
- [ ] The quote is exact if presented as a quote.
- [ ] The number is traced to the original dataset, report, filing, or calculation.
- [ ] The source is appropriate: primary source where possible, reputable secondary source where not.
- [ ] I checked whether later updates, corrections, appeals, errata, or policy changes exist.
- [ ] I removed any source I could not verify.

## Higher-Risk Sources

Use extra care with:

- legal cases, statutes, regulations, and professional rules
- medical, health, safety, tax, finance, investment, HR, and compliance claims
- market statistics and survey numbers
- academic citations and quotations
- security guidance
- client-specific facts
- code or formulas that affect production systems or decisions
